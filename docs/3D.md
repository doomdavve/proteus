# 3D scene foundation

The boating game (`wake_velocity`) renders the basin and the mapped river. Its scenery, actors
and camera are evaluated into one `WaterfrontSceneFrame` shared by both renderers:
`waterfront_scene_evaluate` fills the static scenery and time-based actors, and `boat_view.c`
adds the camera, player and other boats. The four-minute Eriksberg demo (`proteus`, scene
`cube`), its flock camera and the storyboard routes were removed on 2026-10-25; they remain in
the git history.

The GPU waterfront (`src/renderer_gpu.c`) is the default renderer. It provides
procedural ground/facade finishes, local lighting, directional shadows, atmosphere,
and planar water reflections. `--renderer cpu` selects the retained software reference,
implemented in `src/renderer3d.c`, which needs no window or GPU context for captures.
Both paths retain nearest-neighbour, integer-scaled presentation. See the
[playing guide](PLAYING.md) for controls and the [plan index](README.md) for current work.

The CPU reference uses mapped geometry, flat colors and facade windows. The following
geometry and frame APIs describe that software renderer; later sections describe
GPU-specific materials and scene features.

The scene uses mapped footprints, an assumed flat quay and building
heights, and authored crane and bridge proxies. The water is compiled from
`assets/geometry/river/water.h`; the land, buildings and bridge load at start from the scenery
pack `assets/geometry/river/scenery.bin` (A3.5i). Normal builds and playback need no network.
The source snapshot, attribution, and height assumptions are recorded in the adjacent
`manifest.json`. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0 (see README).
GPU facade textures, shadows, atmosphere, and planar water reflection are described below;
detailed terrain remains out of scope.

## Static map storage

The GPU owns each map's static meshes in one interleaved vertex buffer with 16-bit indices, and
`src/renderer_gpu.c` frees the builder's arrays after upload. `WaterfrontScenery`, which the CPU
renderer, minimap and collision read, is a separate copy that this storage never releases. Object
meshes keep raylib's separate arrays, because culling reads them and flags rewrite theirs.

A surface stores only what its shader reads: see `StaticSurface`. If a shader starts reading
another attribute on a surface, such as a texture on a white material or lighting on windows,
change that surface's class in the same step, or it will read the default attribute value.
Normals are renormalized by the shader and must stay so. Hard shadow tests make normal precision
visible: 10-bit normals flipped AA samples, so lit surfaces use 16 bits. See the
[G2a result](GRAPHICS_OVERHAUL_PLAN.md#g2a--geometry-storage-and-ownership) for sizes and bounds.

## Gameplay shadow coverage

Gameplay uses two directional depth maps centred on the camera's look-at target. The
1024×1024 near map covers a 96 m square in light space; a 512×512 far map covers 768 m.
Each map independently snaps its centre to its texel grid to stabilize movement. These
are sun-view dimensions, not fixed distances ahead along the water. Both maps use the
same caster geometry, and each culls tiles against its own volume. The far light camera
sits 1,000 m from its target with a 2,200 m depth span (near: 600 m / 1,400 m), keeping
the wider ground footprint inside the depth volume; its bias preserves the near map's
world-space distance.

City materials and water blend from near to far across 70–95% of the near map's half
extent. The far shadow fades to lit across 80–98% of its half extent, avoiding another
hard cutoff. Nearby shadows retain their original resolution and material strength;
distant shadows are coarser (1.5 m per texel). A depth bias alone cannot cover a far texel: walls
the sun grazed shadowed themselves in thin, jagged stripes that crowded with distance (worst at
sunset, also speckling distant facades by day). City materials therefore look the far map up from
a point moved out along the surface normal by up to one far texel, the full texel when the light
grazes and none when it hits head-on. The near map keeps its depth bias alone. The title and non-player city fixture retain
the original single 500 m map. The fixture's `--shadow-resolution` and `--shadow-map`
diagnostics still refer to the near map.

The far map adds one caster pass per gameplay frame and a 512×512 depth texture (about
0.75–1 MiB for 24/32-bit depth). Existing shadow-pass statistics sum both maps. The
production shader's controlled-depth coverage/blending regression runs in
`gpu_fixture --city --check`; [performance evidence](BOATING_PERFORMANCE.md#distant-shadows--2026-10-31)
records the local comparison and remaining limits.

## Adding geometry

- Define a `Mesh3D` with model-space vertices and indexed `Triangle3D` faces. Each triangle
  has a flat base color; vertices and triangles stay owned by the caller. These types and
  `Transform3D` live in the data-only `src/geometry3d.h`, which simulation code may include;
  only renderers include `renderer3d.h`.
- Use Y-up world coordinates and outward-facing, counterclockwise triangle winding.
- Supply a `Transform3D` with positive scale, Euler rotation in radians (raymath XYZ), and
  world position. Scale is applied before rotation and translation.
- Call `renderer3d_draw_mesh`, or use `renderer3d_draw_box` with a color. The shared box spans
  -0.5 to +0.5, so scale gives its full dimensions. A building of height H rests on Y=0 when
  its center is at Y=H/2.

## Rendering a frame

Fill the color framebuffer with the background, then call `renderer3d_begin` with a look-at
camera and a world-space direction toward the light. Camera FOV is vertical and in radians;
near and far distances must be positive and ordered. The viewing direction and up vector
must be nonzero and nonparallel. Draw only if begin succeeds.

The renderer transforms each triangle, culls back faces, shades using its world-space normal,
clips against all six frustum planes, and projects into the virtual framebuffer. Lighting
is an ambient floor plus one directional light, times a tint (`Renderer3D.ambient`, `diffuse`
and `tint`; begin restores daytime .30, .70 and white). Unlit colours are multiplied by
`emissive_tint`. Normals are calculated after
transformation to support nonuniform scales. Reciprocal view depth is interpolated per pixel,
so intersecting objects do not need sorting. Opaque geometry is supported; transparency,
shadows and wireframe overlays are not implemented. Exactly coplanar surfaces
retain the first submitted fragment; offset markings or model them as separate surfaces.

`Renderer3D` must initially be zeroed. It owns a float depth buffer that grows when needed,
is reused across frames and resolution changes, and is cleared by begin. At 320x240 it uses
307,200 bytes in addition to the color framebuffer. `renderer3d_destroy` releases it;
its owner (for example `wake_velocity`) calls this at exit. Meshes are small and transformed per
triangle for simplicity; profile before adding caches for a larger city.
`renderer.stats` (reset by begin) counts the frame's mesh/box submissions, submitted and
camera-facing triangles, clipped triangles handed to the rasterizer and culled boxes. The GPU
scene keeps the matching `WaterfrontGpuScene.stats`: GL draw submissions, instances and triangles
per pass (shadow, reflection, main) and group (map, water, objects), the batched sky quads and
the tile meshes each pass culled. Counting never changes pixels. Objects drawn through `draw_life`
and `draw_scenery` are instanced in contiguous same-mesh runs; the scalar path remains the
reference and fallback, and `instancing_parity` requires identical bytes from both.

The river's land is culled per tile (adventure A2.3). `WaterfrontScenery` holds a land-only
`BoatMapTiles` catalogue (2,048 m tiles by default since the outer follow-up to P3, 1,024 m
before; larger tiles draw more triangles but fewer submissions, which cost more here). Each land
triangle belongs to the tile
holding its centroid. The CPU reference tests each tile's land box with
`renderer3d_box_visible()` (all eight corners outside one clip plane) and draws the
triangles of each visible tile with `renderer3d_draw_mesh_triangles()`. The GPU builds its
map classes (city, facades, roofs, quay, windows, corner and corner emissive) as
`WaterfrontGpuTiled`: one mesh and box per non-empty tile. Before each draw, it tests the box
in clip space against the pass's own model-view-projection, so the shadow, reflection and
main passes cull against their own volumes. Facade pairs, windows and corner boxes stay in
one tile. Water, the crane and the bridge remain single meshes. Order within a class and tile
is kept, so culling never changes pixels. Drawing classes tile by tile can flip depth ties
between coplanar faces in different tiles. `Renderer3D.culling_disabled` and
`waterfront_gpu_scene_set_culling_enabled()` turn culling off for review.

## Distance detail

Windows and street furniture hold about three quarters of the river's map triangles, in mostly
sub-pixel pieces at range. Those two classes (graphics G2b) therefore sit on a finer grid of
512 m cells (256 m until the outer follow-up, 2026-11-04) and have two levels per cell; the
other classes keep the render tile. The far window level merges up to two columns on two floors into one quad of the same lit area in their mean colour;
the far furniture level drops boxes under 2 m in every direction. Lamps and other emissive boxes,
buildings, the crane, the bridge and the terrain have one level, so landmarks, bridge openings,
shore silhouettes and lit identity stay present.

Each view picks a cell's level from its horizontal distance to the followed boat (or the camera,
without a player): far when 4 m of window spacing fits one presented pixel, about 770 m at
320x200 and 380 m in the 160x120 reflection. A cell switches 8% beyond that distance and returns
8% inside it. That band prevents flicker while holding at a threshold; there is no cross-fade or
temporal blending, so a switch is one frame. Taller output moves the distance out, and supersampling
does not (`detail_scale`), because the presented pixel is what hides the change. Orbiting the camera
around the boat changes no level. The near shadow map draws near, and the far map's 1.5 m texels
always draw far. The state is presentation-only and restarts when a map binds.

`--no-far-detail` in `wake_velocity`, `--no-detail` in `boat_water_review` and
`waterfront_gpu_scene_set_detail_enabled()` draw the near level everywhere, for comparison.

The shadow maps stay uncached: see
[the G2b result](GRAPHICS_OVERHAUL_PLAN.md#g2b--distance-detail-and-valid-shadow-reuse).

## Stamped wake shading

Object wake pieces (player, rival and traffic pools; `boat_wake_objects.c`) feed the water shaders
in three ways (graphics G3a):

- **Height** (`objectWake().x`): the vertex shader adds it to the displaced surface, and the
  fragment shader uses it to gate crest foam (`wakeHeight / 0.3`) and in the patch fade's
  slope term.
- **Slope** (`objectWake().yz`, world X/Z): fragment only. It sets the normal, which drives
  the brightness term, reflection offset and sun highlight. Unlike the legacy path, object
  slopes skip the dock exposure scaling.
- **Crest foam** (`objectCrestFoam`): fragment only. The narrow, broken band on each crest,
  clamped and mixed in at 0.65.

Hull physics reads the same pieces on the CPU (`boat_wake_objects_query`), never from the GPU.

The fragment shader now samples these sums instead of looping over every piece per pixel.
`waterfront_wake_stamp.[ch]` owns an RGBA16F field of 1024 by 1024 texels of 0.25 m (256 m,
8 MB) around the player. Its origin snaps to whole texels, so texels stay fixed to the water as
the boat moves. Each draw lists every live piece's support as one or two quads on the CPU and
adds them into the cleared field with `waterfront_wake_stamp.vs/.fs`, one shader evaluation per
covered texel. Identical records keep the previous field, for example the rear-view mirror's
second draw. The water shader samples the field bilinearly inside a half-texel guard. Outside
it, the shader evaluates only the pieces the CPU found reaching past the guard, listed in a third
row of the record texture, so distant wakes keep their exact shading. Nothing is read back.

At load, a probe adds a signed value twice into a 1x1 RGBA16F target and checks the sum on the
GPU. Renderable float targets alone do not prove float blending. If the probe fails, there is no
field and the shader keeps the analytic loop everywhere. `--analytic-wakes` in `boat_water_review`
and `waterfront_gpu_scene_set_wake_stamp_enabled()` select that loop for comparison.

The vertex shader still loops over every piece for height at each near-patch vertex: up to
38,864 patch triangles a frame in the four-boat stress fixture. G3a leaves it analytic, so hulls
keep agreeing with the physical surface. See
[the G3a result](GRAPHICS_OVERHAUL_PLAN.md#g3a--stamped-wake-shading) for bounds and costs.

## Foam history and ripples

`waterfront_water_history.[ch]` (graphics G3b) keeps cosmetic state that the stamped field
cannot: foam that lingers after a crest passes, and small ripples. Two RGBA16F ping-pong
targets of 512 by 512 texels of 0.25 m (128 m around the player) hold foam (R), ripple height
(G) and the height one step earlier (B); an RGBA8 mask marks water. Together they take 5 MB.
`waterfront_water_history.vs/.fs` advances them on fixed 60 Hz substeps of the frame's
simulated time:

- **Time.** The substep index is `floor(time * 60)`. An unchanged time (pause, or the mirror's
  second draw) runs nothing. A draw runs at most four substeps and drops older time. The first
  draw, a rewind, a jump of more than 0.5 s, a centre moving more than 32 m, a change of hull
  count or a new map resets the state and runs 60 substeps of pre-roll from the current inputs,
  so the same inputs always give the same state.
- **Scrolling.** The origin moves in blocks of 16 texels (4 m). The first substep after a move
  reads the previous state shifted by whole texels; strips that enter the field start empty, and
  the mask is redrawn.
- **Foam.** Each substep moves foam by a small drift, halves it every 1.1 s, and keeps the larger
  of that and the stamped crest foam (crest foam times `height / 0.3`, as the water shader shows
  it). Seeding by maximum instead of adding means foam cannot build into a carpet. The water
  shader shows it as strands, through a fixed world pattern, stretched along the drift, that sets
  how much foam a spot needs; it tears foam into streaks about 2 m apart rather than round cells.
- **Ripples.** A damped wave equation at 3 m/s (Courant number 0.2), with heights clamped to
  ±0.08 m so injected energy stays bounded. Height is held at zero outside the mask and inside
  hull discs (1.3 m around the player and rivals), so quays and hulls reflect ripples. Sources:
  pulses beside both shoulders of each moving hull, phased by distance run so the trail forms
  wavelets; anything bobbing in the surface (`contacts`; today's gulls never fly low enough, so
  only fixtures use it); and drops placed by a hash of texel and substep under the squall's
  rain footprint.
- **Shading.** The water shader adds the ripple slope to the normal (fading it where a pixel
  covers more than a wavelength) and mixes in history foam, both fading out over the last 8 m of
  the field. Heights stay analytic; nothing here reaches physics or the vertices.

The mask is drawn from the bound map's water mesh seen from above. The basin has no water mesh,
so there every texel is open and the basin walls do not reflect ripples. Without float render
targets (the G3a probe) there is no history and the water draws stamp-only.
`waterfront_water_history_tuning()` names the tunables; `--no-water-history` in
`boat_water_review` and `waterfront_gpu_scene_set_water_history_enabled()` give the stamp-only
comparison.

## Place-aware water

Graphics G4 gives the GPU water a sense of place. All of it is presentation only: physics, the
server and the multiplayer content digest never read it, and the CPU renderer does not use it.

- **Water atlas.** `assets/geometry/<map>/water_atlas.bin` is a sidecar baked from each map's
  compiled water by `tools/water_atlas_bake.c` (format and units in `src/boat_water_atlas.h`):
  RGBA8 texels of 4 m (river) or 8 m (outer) holding shore distance, an estimated depth (a bank
  profile of shore distance, not a survey), shelter (how much land closes in) and chop (upwind
  fetch across nine rays, blurred over about 24 m so pier corners leave no steps). It records a
  key of the water it was baked from; a different key, a malformed file or a missing file loads
  nothing, and the water keeps the hand-written dock exposure. Regenerate after the compiled
  water changes, and check the committed file in CTest (`water_atlas_<map>`):

  ```sh
  ./build/water_atlas_bake --map river --out assets/geometry/river/water_atlas.bin \
    --preview build/atlas-river.png
  ```

- **Exposure.** The water shader maps chop onto the old dock field's range (0.18 in a closed dock
  to 1 on open water) and calms it where land closes in. Exposure scales ripple detail, tint and
  reflection strength as before; estimated shallows and the last metres before a shore lighten
  slightly, and history ripples read clearer on sheltered water. The atlas blends back to the
  hand-written field over its outermost 1%.
- **Quay contact.** Just before the water draws, the main pass's depth is blitted into a separate
  depth texture. The water reads it to find quay walls just below the surface and lays a broken,
  slowly moving light band about 2 m wide along them, out to about 400 m. Only geometry behind the
  surface counts, so nothing in front of the water shades it. The scene has no river bed, so open
  water has nothing behind it; drawing outside a render texture keeps no copy. The reflection
  pass draws no water. When the presented frame is wider than 320 pixels (high resolution), the
  band and its light are held to thirds so they stay hard-edged pixel art rather than soft blots;
  low resolution, including supersampled low resolution, keeps the smooth band.
- **Far-water life.** Gust patches of tens of metres drift downwind over exposed water, darker and
  matte with more ripple detail. Sun glitter lights single pixels inside the path toward the sun
  where short waves throw the sun into the eye; it fades in the nearest 35 m and under a grown
  squall. The scene light sets its character (`glint_sparkle`, `glint_speed`, `glint_sheen`):
  since 2026-11-03, daytime keeps about half the sparkles, at about half speed, and fades them
  out where a pixel covers more than a few facets, because there they flickered like static
  without supersampling. A low sun adds a road of light: the sun mirrored in a calmed surface,
  narrow across and long toward the horizon, in three hard brightness steps, broken by slow
  dashes that drift toward the viewer. A weather squall ruffles the water beneath it about as much as a gust; it adds no
  shadow of its own, since the A3.5 storm light already greys the whole frame. All of it comes from
  CPU-reduced phases of world time and the existing squall, and oblique waves under a coordinate
  warp keep the patterns from forming a lattice.

`boat_water_review` takes `--no-water-atlas`, `--no-scene-depth` and `--no-far-water` on any GPU
run, and `--place-clip DIR FRAMES calm|squall` renders an outer-harbour crossing with all G4 water
beside the G3b water. In the game, F8 steps the GPU water back layer by layer (no far-water life,
G3 only, stamp only, analytic wakes).

## Indexed textures and moving light

Use `renderer3d_draw_textured_box` with a `Material3D`. The caller owns the texture and
palette. Texels are indices 0–15; the 256-color palette is arranged as 16 lighting levels
of 16 albedo colors (`palette[level * 16 + texel]`). Non-emissive materials select a ramp
level from the frame's `ambient + diffuse * N.L` plus bounded point light, then apply the
frame's `tint` once after selection (the palette already carries brightness). Emissive
materials select the brightest level and follow the frame's `emissive_tint`, not sun or fill.
This replaces the earlier hardcoded `.16 + .12 * N.L` textured-box light, so a non-emissive
textured box now matches the untextured CPU light and can be brighter at the default
ambient/diffuse. The waterfront's only production textured boxes are emissive street and
navigation lamps. Navigation lamps deliberately keep their authored colours under a non-white
frame unlit tint, so night readability and the GPU emitter semantics are preserved; other
emissive textures follow the unlit tint. The waterfront otherwise uses flat colors with unlit
facade windows and lamps.

Textures repeat with nearest-neighbour sampling at a specified number of texels per world
unit. Mapping selects a world-aligned plane from the dominant face normal. This suits static
architecture; rotating objects would need model-space mapping or UVs to avoid texture sliding.
World position is reconstructed from reciprocal depth after the depth test, so mapping and
lighting remain perspective-correct through frustum clipping. Texture mipmaps are not yet
implemented; fine distant detail can shimmer in motion.

After `renderer3d_begin`, set `renderer.point_light` with a world-space position, positive
radius and intensity. Begin disables the previous frame's point light. Textured surfaces use
ambient and directional fill plus normal-facing point illumination with squared distance
falloff inside the radius, quantized to a palette level. The point light does not affect the
legacy flat-color mesh path. Lights do not cast shadows or test occlusion; emissive surfaces
do not automatically emit light. The waterfront uses directional shading plus depth-tested,
stepped warm light patches under street lamps and ahead of cars. These patches are stylized illumination, without
shadow casting or light spill onto other surfaces.

Keep composition and animation in the scene, and projection and visibility in the renderer.
Texture mipmaps and shadow-casting lights are not implemented in the CPU reference.
GPU shadows and material filtering are separate features described below. Profile before
extending the software renderer.

`validate_renderer3d` checks intersection draw order, perspective depth, near-plane crossing,
work counters,
frustum rejection, camera translation, depth reset on resize, indexed texture mapping,
ambient/diffuse/tint level selection, point-light falloff/facing and emissive-tint
independence. For visual checks:

```sh
./build/wake_velocity --renderer cpu --location outer --screenshot build/river-cpu.png --time 3
./build/wake_velocity --renderer cpu --location outer --screenshot build/river-start.png --time 0
```

Capture several times and both renderers when changing materials or lighting. Profile optimized builds when deciding how much detail or lighting to add.

## Waterfront development

### Boat-level water

Playable GPU waves use an exact shoreline-clipped near patch on a fixed world grid,
with full displacement near the hull and a smooth transition to flat distant geometry.
Both surfaces share analytic wave normals and one boating albedo. The dock-to-river
exposure field changes shading only; physical waves and boat handling retain the
shared `boat_water_query` model. The CPU renderer retains the full tessellated reference,
displacing only the water triangles in view. A patch rebuild and that visibility cut both use
the cache's 256 m water index (adventure A2.4) and give the same triangles, in the same order,
as a full-map pass.
See [the 04f implementation record](WATER_REVIEW.md#04f-implementation-boat-level-surface-and-bounded-geometry)
for distances, memory/frame-cost measurements, and before/after captures.

### Generated boats and berths

`src/boat_model.[ch]` generates caller-owned, deterministic meshes from hull/cabin/rigging/
engine/color recipes. `src/waterfront_harbor.[ch]` caches seven moored boat meshes plus
pontoon and quay equipment. The shared frame snapshot carries their wave poses, gangway,
and attached mooring lines. The CPU renderer draws the same geometry; GPU resources upload
once and participate in color, reflection, and shadow passes. The basin course and existing
player mesh remain unchanged. See [the boat generator and berth guide](HARBOR_BOATS.md)
for parameter contracts, limitations, review views, and the later playable-boat boundary.

### Boating gulls

`waterfront_boating_gulls` evaluates 32 birds from explicit time and the player's renderer-space
position/velocity. The boat snapshot and CPU boating path use the same evaluator. GPU birds
are drawn before the player branch returns, including in reflection and shadow passes.
See [harbor gulls](HARBOR_GULLS.md) for cues and limits.

## GPU ground materials

Road/path surfaces and parking bays use quiet asphalt; the authored raised pavements use
80 x 40 cm staggered pavers with 2 cm joints. Open quay concrete uses 3 m construction
panels with 2.5 cm expansion joints, while planted beds use irregular clump/soil variation.
These are procedural, world-space finishes in `assets/shaders/waterfront_city.fs`, not
larger bitmap tiles. Surface identity comes from the retained mesh/static kit palettes
and upward-facing ground-level geometry; tree crowns, walls, furniture, and roofs are
excluded. The importer currently gives mapped roads and footpaths the same source colour;
the separate paving finish applies to the authored pavement geometry, not all mapped paths.

The existing ground batch (`WaterfrontGpuScene.quay`) stores a constant
`WaterfrontGroundMaterial` ID per triangle in `texcoord.x`. Its draw enables `groundPass`
and resets that uniform before other meshes. Authored pavement tops join this batch instead
of being drawn twice. Main and reflection passes share it; shadow geometry is unchanged.
This adds no draw calls, textures, or geometry. `quay.png` remains a legacy asset but is no
longer loaded. Roof bitmap mapping is unchanged; facades have their own finish pass below.

Screen-space derivatives determine each pixel's world footprint. Seams integrate their
coverage across that footprint and unresolved detail fades to its average. Thus small
joints do not become oversized dark pixels or a distant repeating carpet. Presentation
still uses nearest-neighbour integer scaling; lighting and the CPU reference are unchanged.

With `PROTEUS_GPU_TESTS=ON`, `gpu_surface_materials` checks all four ground material IDs, homogeneous
upward triangles, near-detail response, and far-view stability under a 2 cm camera shift.
The probe uses neutral colours and controlled illumination to isolate material behaviour.
Run it directly with `./build/gpu_fixture --city --check` (graphics context required).

### GPU facade materials

The old `facade.png` painted-window tile is no longer loaded: the existing window mesh
is the only source of openings and room colours. The retained per-building palette selects
red/buff brick, warm plaster, or cool panels consistently across walls and mapped parts.
These material assignments are artistic, not surveyed building finishes.

Facade UVs are metres along each wall and height above the 3.5 m quay datum. Both triangles
of an imported wall share the same origin; angled walls therefore retain the same material
scale without stretching or diagonal seams. Brick uses 26 x 9 cm courses with 9 mm mortar;
panels use 1 m modules and 3.2 m floor spacing coordinated with the existing window rows.
Plaster has restrained grain and floor bands. A low masonry base anchors the walls. All
fine patterns use the same footprint filtering as the ground, leaving palette and broad
structure to carry the distant image instead of oversized individual bricks.

The existing facade batch stores RGB albedo and a `WaterfrontFacadeMaterial` ID in vertex
alpha. This is metadata, not transparency: `facadePass` always outputs opaque fragments,
and is enabled/reset around main/reflection facade draws. Shadow geometry is unchanged.
No extra mesh submissions, triangles, or textures are added. Roofs, windows, lighting,
ground finishes, actors, camera, and CPU rendering are unchanged.

`gpu_surface_materials` (formerly `gpu_ground_materials`) also checks facade family coverage,
metre scale on angled faces, shared-corner UV continuity, material identity across wall
triangles, opacity, distinct material response, and near/far filtering. Run
`./build/gpu_fixture --city --check` with a graphics context.

### Static city dressing

The GPU city includes an authored cafe at the northwest corner of mapped building
282574767 beside Monsungatan. Its raised pavement, striped awning, KAFFE sign, warm
display windows, entrance, tables/chairs, planters, bench, bin, menu board, and two parked
bicycles establish the first detailed street corner. The cafe is an artistic addition,
not a claim about a real business at that address.

`src/waterfront_corner.[ch]` now also distributes the kit across exterior facades, both
streets, and the dock banks: entrances, smaller shops, pavements, parking, planted shared
courts, benches, bicycles, quay rails, and bollards. Surface-height footprint checks avoid
mapped roads, roofs, gardens, and water; the cafe and crane supports are reserved. These
placements are artistic, not surveyed. Local frames follow each facade or quay edge.
Each CPU/GPU view owns its dressing as a `WaterfrontScenery` built from the bound map
(adventure A1.4); the cafe and street kit belong only to the compiled river crop.
The GPU uploads two batches when its scene is bound (opaque and emissive), reuses them
in the reflection pass, and includes opaque props in the directional shadow pass. A
reserved shop light uses the existing 32-light budget; all street lamps remain eligible

## Authored boating water profiles

`boat_water_query` is shared by buoyancy, foam attachment, CPU surface generation, and
scripted-source cues. Its sample contains surface height, simulation-space normal, and
material surface velocity. Optional horizontal flow comes from a uniform/compact current
field; vertical velocity includes both wave time derivatives and travel along slopes.
`boat_water_displaced` selects tessellated water for ambient waves or a crossing wake,
including the wake-only preset. GPU vertex displacement and fragment slope/crest shading
use the same bounded wake recipes; a readback test compares them with the CPU query.
The opt-in native basin `--wake-field` instead uploads a fixed CPU height field and uses the
same degree-eleven interpolant for CPU height/gradient and GLSL shading. Its full-basin
.75 m indexed mesh has no camera-distance displacement fade. The owner also supplies
potential-derived horizontal velocity and endpoint vertical velocity to the hull. This is
separate from the legacy D3 path below; [candidate results](WAKE_CANDIDATE_RESULTS.md) own
accuracy, storage, cost, still-water scope and pending play acceptance.

For `--player-wake`, `BoatWater` owns 64 travelled segments. The renderer packs live
segments into two vec4 uniform arrays, while the water query evaluates the same compact
packets and smooth overlap normalization. The packet spreads at 1.6 units/s while crests
travel at 3 units/s; spatial/time derivatives account for both speeds. Copies own their
history; no global mutable
wake source is shared between a player, preview, or captured frame.
The free-boating default, explicitly selected with `--wake-objects`, borrows an app-owned `BoatWakeObjects` world
through `BoatWater.objects`. Four emitters have thirty-two independent ridge slots each;
relative integer-tick age controls birth, motion and expiry. CPU contact adds their signed
height, analytic slope and vertical motion. The GPU reads a two-row 4096-byte object-record texture with
an explicit object-mode selector and the same ridge polynomial in both shader stages;
D3 keeps its 64-entry uniform arrays. There is no dense height upload. The existing
clipped near patch bounds detailed rendering,
while off-camera objects retain their age until expiry. Copies borrow the world and must
not outlive it. [Object results](WAKE_OBJECT_RESULTS.md) own controls, cost and verification.
The near/far representation retains its behavior. Free-boating launch settings now enable
player wakes by default; zero-initialized API settings and `--no-player-wake` with no other
water flags retain flat, still water. Mapped scripted sources translate the complete CPU/GPU
field into a checked open-river corridor. Current streaks use shoreline masking and the
same shared scene/CPU visitor in every location.
See [physics Phase D](BOAT_PHYSICS_PLAN.md#phase-d--currents-and-crossing-wakes) for

## Scene lighting

`src/waterfront_lighting.[ch]` owns one `WaterfrontLighting` value per frame: sun direction,
GPU ambient/direct light, CPU flat-shading terms, the sky panorama's colours, haze, water
(body, sun shadow, glitter, highlight), window light and a multiplier for self-coloured
surfaces such as gulls and traffic wakes. `waterfront_lighting(phase)` evaluates the original
daylight contract from an explicit evening phase, never the wall clock: 0 is the current
Day look (the zero default), 0.5 sunset and 1 blue dusk, with linear blends between
them. The scene frame carries it (`WaterfrontSceneFrame.lighting`, daytime from evaluation).

`waterfront_lighting_hour(hour, &lighting)` is the separate full-day evaluator. Finite hours
wrap into `[0,24)` (`24` and `-0` become 0, `-1` is 23, `25` is 1); non-finite input fails and
leaves the caller's frame untouched. The artistic anchors are 0 night, 4.5 dawn, 6 sunrise,
9 morning, 12 day, 15 afternoon, 18 sunset, 19.5 dusk and 21.5 night, with hour 24 repeating
night at the seam. Elevation and azimuth are interpolated as angles, sunrise and sunset face
opposite sides, and the sun stays below the zenith. Direct light, sun disc, glitter, highlight
and cast water shadow share a smooth horizon gate: at or below the horizon they are zero and
the water is fully unshadowed. Night and dawn have their own recipes, not a darkened dusk. Day,
sunset and dusk reuse the approved palettes at their anchor hours but with the new sun bearing;
the user approved the T1 preview on 2026-11-07. Broader renderer/readability qualification
remains T2 work; the legacy functions and named looks remain exact.

`waterfront_grade_hour(hour, &grade)` returns the neutral HDR grade for a full-day hour without
passing that hour to `waterfront_grade(phase)`. `BoatGpu` and `BoatCpuView` carry the legacy
`daylight` phase plus a view-owned `hour` and `hour_set`; a zero-initialized view clears
`hour_set` and therefore keeps the old Day default. `BoatViewProps` may instead carry a
`lighting_cycle` with unwrapped hours and a period; both renderers resolve one normalized hour
from it. The game disables the cycle property for explicit fixed CLI overrides. `--lighting-hour H` selects the fixed
full-day override in `wake_velocity` and `boat_water_review`; it overrides `--daylight` regardless
of argument order. In `wake_velocity`, `--time` remains scripted drive and wave time, independent
of the light; `--time-scale` changes how many fixed ticks run per real frame in solo live play,
so the Working Harbour clock and world scale together. Review tools retain their pose ticks and
clip frames. They report `lighting_mode`, `daylight` and `lighting_hour` separately.

`boat_water_review --lighting-qualify PREFIX` audits a fixed hour against a sampled virtual
900 s cycle on the same independent pose trajectory, with production scene drawing and honest
CPU/GPU timing and counters; see the [lighting plan](SUNSET_PLAN.md#t2c-implementation-2026-11-07).
The rear-view mirror follows the selected light on both renderers: its CPU view receives only
`daylight`/`hour`/`hour_set` via `boat_rearview_set_lighting()`, and its 30 Hz cache includes
renderer and light so a same-wave-time light change refreshes the mirror. Fixed-hour seeks are
history independent. The T4 cycle adds a seekable glitter phase (the analytic integral below)
because the legacy GPU glitter path multiplies time by the instantaneous `glint_speed`.

Both renderers read the frame, not constants: GPU sky, city/facade/ground lighting, fog,
shadow direction, water, reflections (drawn with the same uniforms) and windows; the CPU sky,
flat shading, windows, displaced water, the basin's flat water and self-coloured actors.

Day uses a deeper blue background: horizon RGB (160, 213, 242), zenith (22, 91, 190),
with a 0.65 elevation span. The lighter horizon retains atmospheric softness. Both
renderers and GPU sky reflections use this recipe. The fixed-hour noon anchor uses
the same background and blends toward the existing morning/afternoon anchors; their
own sky palettes and gradient spans remain explicit. Cloud colours, fog and scene-light
values remain independent of this background adjustment.

The fair-weather sky retains a shallow cloud deck with a visible underside and thin rim.
Its broad world-X/Z patches use smoothly interpolated deterministic noise with a small
amount of finer edge detail. A second noise field at 4.5 times the patch scale
modulates density to gather patches into banks with wider clear areas between them. Sampling is continuous before the binary mask threshold,
which gives the deck irregular curved boundaries instead of coarse square tile steps.
A 0.68 footprint threshold leaves fewer patches than the earlier 0.60 recipe. Three
shading terraces use an offset sample of the same field (thresholds 0.74 and 0.80),
giving broad shadow, middle and light regions that remain legible in water reflections.
The offset avoids centered contour rings. The shadow uses 92% of the rim colour, the
middle mixes rim/underside by 45%, and the light uses 103.5% of the underside colour;
the exposed thin rim uses the light colour. These are stylized static terraces, not
new moving cloud geometry.
The fixed mask remains crisp at native resolution; the two layer samples retain the
existing perspective and apparent thickness. This is not a cumulus-volume renderer.

The CPU keeps a full 1024x512 panorama reference in separate storage: `waterfront_sky_image()`
repaints it when another light's sky differs (`waterfront_lighting_same_sky`). The production
CPU draw no longer repaints that panorama. `waterfront_sky_draw_squall()` ray-maps visible
pixels to the same panorama texel centres and fills a bounded lazy cache with the shared
`sky_texel` recipe, using the light-independent `waterfront_sky_cloud_mask()` (R underside, G lit rim,
B terrace values 85/170/255 on the underside and zero outside) so static
cloud hashes are not recomputed per sample. The cache holds one sky's colours plus a `uint32`
generation stamp per texel (about 4 MiB, static, no per-frame heap); a changed sky starts a new
generation, so old texels are never used, nothing large is cleared, and each visible texel is
evaluated at most once. Repeated draws under a fixed light do no recipe work; alternating lights
and direct/backward seeks re-evaluate only the visible texels. The weather squall is still
applied per ray after the cached base sky. `waterfront_sky_stats()` reports reference paints,
visible misses and cache hits for tests. Reference and draw use the same `sky_texel` recipe but
independent storage, caching and traversal; the captured parent images remain the independent
recipe baseline, not a separate mathematical reference. The GPU uploads only the static mask
once per scene, then `assets/shaders/waterfront_sky.fs` evaluates the same gradient,
glow, halo, disc, cloud colours and fixed grain from `WaterfrontLighting` uniforms at the
original panorama texel centres. A changing hour therefore updates uniforms, not a sky
texture; main, reflection and mirror passes share the frame's light values. For a fixed light,
the glitter's sparkle speed scales the CPU-reduced wave phase rate. A cycle instead supplies
`frame.glitter_time` from `waterfront_glitter_clock()`, an analytic integral of the same
anchored `glint_speed` smoothstep recipe, zero at noon. It walks the anchors once per query
(O(anchor count)), supports direct seeks and keeps no history; the legacy fixed path still uses
wave time multiplied by the instantaneous speed. The earlier panorama painter measured about
25 ms in Debug; continuous CPU transitions now pay visible-sample work, with fixed and
changing-hour measurements in the [T2b result](SUNSET_PLAN.md#t2b-implementation-2026-11-07).
Windows keep their daytime glass; `waterfront_window_occupied()` picks
about three in eight per wall triangle and window, shared by both renderers, and the GPU
windows pass carries that in vertex alpha (far blocks: the occupied share) and draws opaque.
At dusk the sun is below the horizon, so direct light, glitter and water shadow are zero
while the shadow maps are still rendered.

The Working Harbour workboat, crane and cargo props on GPU now follow the frame's CPU flat
light and tint instead of a fixed key light. A bounded work-only ambient floor,
`max(cpu_ambient, .8 * window_light)`, makes the work solids and landing decal readable at
night without changing hue, direct sun or the approved global lighting; Day has
`window_light = 0`, so it is unchanged. The standalone `cargo_sandbox` harness deliberately
keeps its fixed-day fixture look.

SETTINGS → LIGHTING cycles 27 fixed choices—DAY (default), SUNSET, DUSK, then 00:00 through
23:00—for the local player's new solo, joined and hosted sessions. The choice persists as exact
legacy names or `hour-v1:HH`; malformed values and unknown versions fall back to Day. Explicit
CLI launches use `--daylight day|sunset|dusk` (default day, or a phase 0..1) or
`--lighting-hour` rather than the saved title choice; the review tools also accept
`--daylight` with `boat_water_review --review` or `--place-clip ... --clip-aa off|4x4`. The CPU
basin now draws the same frame sky and `boat_view_squall` as the mapped scene, begins with the
evaluated `sun_direction` and uses the shared `cpu_ambient`, `cpu_direct` and `cpu_tint`. Both CPU
flat-water paths (the basin body and the mapped map water) apply the frame's water multiplier
once around the flat-water submission together with that ordinary ambient/direct/tint shading,
so the base water colours are unchanged and a day factor of one keeps the default mapped water.
Displaced water keeps its existing emissive water-tint path. This intentionally changes basin
captures from earlier daytime-only builds; physics and the physical wave model are unchanged.
The CPU water remains a simplified flat shade: it has no GPU water atlas, reflection, glitter
or the GPU's per-map water treatment, so matching factor policy is not pixel parity.
`waterfront_lighting` quickly checks preset exactness, smooth blends, reference repaint/restore,
window occupancy and parsing; the exhaustive `--sky-check` comparison is the separate slow
`waterfront_lighting_sky` test. `boat_world_isolation.cpu_basin_lighting` covers the basin sky,
hour wrap/seek and both flat-water tint paths, and is registered slow because it paints the
full reference.

The [lighting plan](SUNSET_PLAN.md#t3-implementation-2026-11-08) owns fixed full-day
evaluation. T1 added the evaluator, fixed CLI override and artistic anchors while keeping the
legacy 0..1 phase, named presets, saved settings and Day default unchanged. T3 player-facing
fixed-hour controls are implemented locally and the user accepted the controls (2026-11-08).
T4's Working Harbour session clock, v2 save and glitter integral are implemented locally with

[GPU upload strategy](SUNSET_PLAN.md#sky-rendering-and-gpu-uploads-t2-prerequisite-for-t4)
is implemented in T2a as a static cloud mask and changing lighting uniforms, so the GPU no
longer repaints or uploads the panorama during a cycle; the CPU sky remains the full
reference. T2b merged in #171 with CPU visible-sample sky work, shared basin light and

## Resolution and HDR presentation

`src/boat_screen.[ch]` decides sizes from the logical frame (320×200, or the browser's mobile
size) and the window. Low resolution, the default, renders the scene at the logical size with a
160×120 reflection, exactly as before. High (`--resolution high`, title PICTURE HIGH RES) renders
it at the window's integer scale of the logical frame, at most 4 and no side above 2,048, with
the reflection grown by the same factor (1280×800 and 640×480 in a 1280×800 window). The HUD,
menus and hit targets keep the logical frame: high draws them into a transparent logical
target with premultiplied alpha (`boat_screen_begin_overlay`) and presents that, nearest and
integer-scaled, over the scene (`boat_screen_present_overlay`). A window larger than four times
the frame filters the scene bilinearly unless its scale is a multiple of 4. High needs the GPU
and replaces `--gpu-aa`. `--frame-capture FILE` saves the presented window at the end of
`--frames`.

HDR (`--hdr on|off`, on by default at high resolution) is owned by `BoatGpu`: the colour pass
renders into an RGBA16F target with a depth buffer (so the water's depth copy still works), and
`assets/shaders/gpu_post.fs` writes the 8-bit target. Bloom takes only light above the grade's
threshold of 1, blurs it through half-, quarter- and eighth-size targets and adds a fifth of it;
`src/waterfront_grade.[ch]` then applies exposure, gain, lift, saturation and contrast and the
image is clamped, with no other tone curve: light above 1 is first scaled back to the clamp with
its hue, so a warm lamp stays warm and only its bloom shows the excess. Legacy grades use the
lighting frame's evening phase; full-day views use `waterfront_grade_hour()` with the explicit
hour. Both paths are neutral for now. By day nothing exceeds 1 (the city shader keeps its
palette clamp, the water glitter mixes toward its colour and the sky is an 8-bit panorama), so HDR changes daytime
by at most one 8-bit step. With window light (sunset and dusk) self-lit surfaces in the main pass
(lit windows, lamp and deck-light lenses, crane and car lights) are multiplied by
`1 + 0.35 * window_light` before haze, which feeds the bloom; the 8-bit reflection keeps them at
1. Without HDR the gain stays 1. The rear-view mirror and
supersampling draw 8-bit directly. Without float blending (the wake stamp's probe), or after a
failed allocation, presentation stays 8-bit; bloom and grading are then off. At 1280×800 the
HDR targets hold 15.0 MB.

## Clustered lights and dusk water

`src/waterfront_lights.[ch]` (G5b) replaces the per-frame choice of 32 lights nearest the shot.
Every frame the GPU scene gathers each local light the frame offers, with a stable ID: the river
cafe (40) and crane lights (10–12), corner-street lamps (100 + street × 100 + index), car
headlights in non-boating frames (1000 + …), the dusk fixtures (2000 + index) and review stress
lights (10000 + index). Lights go into a world-space grid of 8 m X/Z cells wrapped onto 64 × 64
list slots, so the lists depend on no camera: the view, its mirrored reflection and the water
shade each point from the same lights, and nothing pops as a view crosses a cell. Each light is
listed in every slot its radius touches; a slot holds 16 lights, and shading tests each light's
distance, so a slot shared by far-apart cells costs work, never correctness. Rank order (higher
intensity × radius, then lower ID) decides what survives the 1,024-light and 16-per-slot limits,
whatever the submission order; the scene records submissions, dropped and clipped lights,
overflowing slots and the busiest slot (`WaterfrontGpuScene.light_stats`).

The lists upload each frame into one 256 × 96 RGBA32F texture on unit 5 (four texels per light,
an offset/count header per slot, then indices four to a texel; the used rows only, 32 for no
indices) that the city and water shaders read with `texelFetch`, which GL 3.3 and WebGL2 provide
without extensions. If the storage or texture cannot be created (or with
`boat_water_review --light-fallback`), the city keeps the earlier capped uniform list: crane,
cafe, the closest lamps and headlights to the shot, up to 32; the water then has no lamplight.
The scene records what that list left out (`fallback_dropped`, including lights behind the eye).

Window light gates everything the water adds, so daytime water is unchanged. With it, the water
shows a faint pool under each nearby light and a glint where the rippled surface throws it to
the eye. Long stepped streaks come from the reflection: warm emitters in it (lit windows, lamps
and lenses; the dusk sky and scenery are blue and the afterglow too dim in green) smear along
the reflection's vertical axis, ten 2-texel steps toward the viewer and four away, broken into
dashes by world-space ripples and held to thirds of full strength. They follow the reflection's
occlusion and coverage, so land, quays and the frame edge mask them. The streaks key on colour,
not an emission mask, so a warm, bright non-emitter in the reflection can streak too.
With window light the harbour also shows deck lights along both sides of the docked ferry and
lamps on the pontoon or authored dock modules, placed on the evaluated harbour poses; their
light scales with the window light. They draw only on the GPU.

`waterfront_lights` checks slots, rank order, order-independence, overflow and capacity, packing,
and that each slot's list finds exactly the lights reaching 20,000 points. The GPU
`light_clusters` check renders the river at dusk and with 600 review lights, at low and high,
and requires the clustered frame to match `--light-reference` (every light tested at every
pixel) byte for byte, also after a rear-view mirror pass; daytime clusters match the fallback.

## Baked ambient occlusion

Graphics G5c darkens creases in the mapped scenery: wall bases, the street along a facade, quay
corners and the underside of the bridge deck. It is presentation only: physics, the server and
the multiplayer content digest never read it, and the CPU renderer does not use it.

- **Sidecar.** `assets/geometry/<map>/ambient.bin` is baked from each map's scenery pack by
  `tools/ambient_bake.c` (format in `src/boat_ambient.h`). It holds one byte per triangle corner:
  how much of the open hemisphere above that corner the pack's other land, wall, building and
  bridge triangles close off within 6 m, from 24 fixed rays with each hit weighted by nearness
  (0 open to 255 closed). Water neither occludes nor carries a value, and no sun, boat, actor or
  run-time kit counts. A facade corner is the corner point alone; any other corner is the mean of
  four points in its third of the triangle, so one creased corner of a long quay wall or a large
  ground triangle does not shade all of it. The file records a key of the pack it was baked from;
  a different key, a malformed file or a missing file loads nothing, and the scenery draws
  unoccluded. Regenerate after a pack changes (the outer map takes about 20 s), and check the
  committed files in CTest (`ambient_<map>`):

  ```sh
  ./build/ambient_bake --map river --out assets/geometry/river/ambient.bin --stats
  ```

- **Shading.** Each map vertex carries its corner value in the spare fourth short of the packed
  normal (1 + value; three-float normals read 1, unoccluded), so the vertex size is unchanged.
  Corners with different values no longer share a vertex, which adds 4.5% river and 2.6% outer
  map vertex bytes. The city shader removes up to `WATERFRONT_GPU_AMBIENT_OCCLUSION` (0.6) of the
  ambient term only; sun, lamps and emitters keep their contact. A facade is one tall triangle
  per wall, so it applies its base corners' value only over the bottom 4 m (by wall metres), as
  contact with the street. Flat facets and authored colours are unchanged.

`boat_water_review --no-ambient-occlusion` draws any GPU run at zero strength; its frames equal
the previous commit's to within one 8-bit step on at most one pixel. `--geometry` reports
`occluded_vertices`.

## Offline geometry preparation

With Python dependencies `pyproj==3.8.0` and `shapely==2.1.2` installed, run:

```sh
python3 tools/prepare_waterfront.py
```

The converter verifies the pinned bridge-survey snapshot hash (`docs/river/osm-survey.json.gz`),
runs triangulation and height checks, and writes `scenery.bin`, `water.h` and `manifest.json` under
`assets/geometry/river/`. The crop covers X −1500…950, Z −1600…800 m.
Mapped building parts partition their parent footprints by height (highest overlapping
part wins); decimal-comma heights and elevated `min_height` parts are supported. Bridge
pylons tagged as building parts are excluded. The manifest records parts and the authored
garden/park zones, whose planting avoids mapped roads, buildings, and water. These are
artistic landscape assumptions, not surveyed terrain. Coarse park canopies establish
scale; street furniture and detailed planting remain later work.
River closing edges are checked to stay outside the crop.
Use `--output build/waterfront-repro` to verify regeneration without overwriting bundled
assets. Normal builds do not run the converter or require these Python dependencies.

The water is cut around the four mapped tower-leg footprints; the bridge deck and
open tower/cable blockout are appended as shared colored geometry by
`tools/prepare_bridge.py`. The same generator connects both deck ends to the mapped inland roads
with descending slabs, grassy embankments, and concrete abutments. Land-only grading corridors
reserve space for the approaches, and the manifest lists displaced building footprints.
A generated triangle-range marker identifies the authored bridge and approaches. Both renderers
exclude that range from facade-window generation; the GPU keeps it in the flat material pass so
the roads and retaining walls receive ordinary lighting, reflections, and shadows without
apartment facade or roof textures.
The river manifest records authored dimensions, omitted incomplete relations, and invalid
zero-height fallbacks. The original, smaller Eriksberg crop and its snapshot were removed on
2026-10-25. Regeneration with this revision of the script has not been rerun.
